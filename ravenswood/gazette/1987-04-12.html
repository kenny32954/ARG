Utility Test Blamed for Brief Radio Interference

Municipal engineers say late-night reception complaints were caused by routine equipment work at the North Feed test yard.

A municipal communications test conducted Saturday night caused several short bursts of interference on household radios, according to a statement released by the town engineering office.

Generators at the North Feed yard were shut down shortly after 11 p.m., but some residents reported hearing a repeated voice-like sound several minutes after crews said the test had ended.

According to engineer A. Hale, the project involved a buried induction loop intended for environmental monitoring and receiver calibration. Hale declined to discuss the audio portion of the test.

Internal records reviewed by the Gazette list no active broadcast transmitter at the yard. A town spokesperson said the interference could have traveled through nearby wiring or older utility lines.

No further testing is scheduled at the North Feed location. Equipment will be transferred to a different municipal parcel later this month, the statement said.

Residents who notice continued interference are encouraged to report the time and receiver type to the communications office. Officials said no public-safety frequencies were affected.
